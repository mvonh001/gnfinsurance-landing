What the check actually covers

  • Your doctors, by name. Not "most doctors in your area" — the specific people you see, checked against each plan's network.
  • Your prescriptions, by name and dose. Drug lists differ by plan and change every year, including for plans you already have.
  • Your pharmacy. Cost for the same drug can differ depending on where you fill it.
  • What's actually sold in your county. Not a national list.

Why the county matters

Medicare Advantage and Part D plans are approved and priced county by county. Two people the same age, with the same doctors and the same prescriptions, living twenty minutes apart, can have completely different options. It's the first thing that has to be right, which is why the ZIP is worth giving even though it's optional.

When you're able to make a change

Enrollment in a plan may be limited to certain times of the year. The main ones:

  • Turning 65. Your Initial Enrollment Period runs seven months: the three months before your birthday month, that month, and the three months after.
  • Annual Enrollment, October 15 to December 7. Anyone with Medicare can change plans for the following year.
  • Special Enrollment Periods. Moving, losing employer coverage, and certain other life events can open a window outside those dates.

If you're outside every window, I'll tell you that on the call rather than waste your time. Knowing the date you can act is worth the conversation on its own.
