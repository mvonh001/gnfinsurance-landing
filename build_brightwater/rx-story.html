Medicare & Your Money

Why So Many Florida Seniors Quietly Overpay for Their Prescriptions

By Michael von Heesen · Licensed Florida Medicare Agent (G345487) · Miami

Before I opened my own agency, I spent seven years on the phone doing one thing: helping people lower the cost of their prescriptions. Thousands of calls. And almost every expensive surprise I saw traced back to the same quiet mechanism most people have never heard explained.

Every drug plan has a list

Every Medicare drug plan keeps a list of the medications it covers, called a formulary, sorted into price tiers. Here is the part that costs people money: every plan's list is different, and the lists change every single year. The same inhaler can cost a few dollars on one plan and a few hundred on another, at the same pharmacy, in the same week.

When your pharmacy bill creeps up, it is usually not the pharmacy, and it is not the medication. It is a plan whose list no longer matches the prescriptions you actually take. It happens to careful people, because checking the match means reading documents nobody enjoys reading.

The ten-minute fix

The fix is not complicated, it is just tedious, which is why I do it for people. You tell me your medications by name. I check them against the drug coverage of the plans I represent in your county: which plan covers your list at the lowest tiers, and whether a different setup would cost less over the year. If your current plan is already your cheapest way to fill your medications, I will tell you exactly that, and the call is over.

The timing matters more than people realize. Drug plan changes generally happen during the fall Annual Enrollment Period, October 15 through December 7. People who check their list now walk into October knowing exactly what to do. People who wait find out in January, at the pharmacy counter, when the new prices start.
