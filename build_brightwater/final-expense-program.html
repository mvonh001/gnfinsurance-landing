So the ads are a scam?

Not exactly, and that is the confusing part. The product behind the ads is usually legitimate: a small whole life insurance policy, typically $2,000 to $40,000, made to cover funeral and final costs. Licensed insurance carriers issue these policies and the State of Florida regulates them.

The misleading part is the wrapper: the "program" language, the government look, the fake deadlines. The biggest catch is what happens to your phone number. Many of those TV and mail offers are run by lead companies, not agents. Call the 800 number and your information often gets sold to several call centers. Then the calls start and they do not stop.

What real final expense insurance is

A small whole life policy. Your rate locks for life. Coverage never expires as long as premiums are paid. It pays cash directly to the person you choose, usually within days, for a funeral, medical bills, or anything else. A typical Florida funeral runs $8,000 to $12,000. Most policies skip the medical exam: approval comes from a short list of health questions.

Red flags to watch for

  • Government look-alikes. Eagles, flags, "state-approved," "new 2026 program." Insurance is state-regulated. There is no enrollment program.
  • Fake deadlines. Final expense insurance has no enrollment period. You can apply any day of the year.
  • Call centers. If the number routes to an agent floor in another state, your information is probably being resold. Ask two questions: are you an agent, and are you licensed in Florida?
  • One price for everyone. Real quotes require your age and health answers.
