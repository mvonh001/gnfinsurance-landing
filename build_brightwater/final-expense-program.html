The "$25,000 Final Expense Program" from TV, explained honestly

You've seen the ads: a "new state-approved program" that pays up to $25,000 for funeral costs, "for pennies a day," with a countdown telling you to call before enrollment closes. Here's what's actually behind them.

By Michael von Heesen, independent licensed insurance agent · FL Lic. G345487 · Bay Harbor Islands, Florida

There is no government or state "$25,000 burial program." What those ads sell is final expense life insurance — a real, useful, state-regulated product — wrapped in marketing designed to look like a government benefit.

So the ads are a scam?

Not exactly — and that's the confusing part. The product behind the ads is usually legitimate: a small whole life insurance policy (typically $2,000–$40,000) designed to cover funeral and final costs. Policies like these are issued by licensed insurance carriers and regulated by the State of Florida.

What's misleading is the wrapper: the "program" language, the government look, the fake deadlines. And the biggest catch is what happens to your phone number — many of those TV and mail offers are run by lead companies, not agents. Call the 800 number and your information is often sold to several call centers who will chase you for weeks.

What real final expense insurance is

A small whole life policy: your rate is locked for life, coverage never expires as long as premiums are paid, and it pays cash directly to the person you choose — typically within days — to cover a funeral (the median Florida funeral runs $8,000–$12,000), medical bills, or anything else. Most policies skip the medical exam; approval is based on a short list of health questions.

Red flags to watch for

  • Government look-alikes. Eagles, flags, "state-approved," "new 2026 program." Insurance is state-regulated; there is no enrollment program.
  • Fake deadlines. "Enrollment closes Friday" — final expense insurance has no enrollment period. You can apply any day of the year.
  • Call centers. If the number routes to a licensed-agent floor in another state, your info is likely being resold. Ask: "Are you an agent, and are you licensed in Florida?"
  • One price for everyone. Real quotes require your age and health answers.
