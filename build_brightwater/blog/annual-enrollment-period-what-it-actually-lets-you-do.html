The Annual Enrollment Period Is Coming: What Oct 15 to Dec 7 Actually Lets You Do

The fall window is a chance to make changes, not a requirement to do anything. Here's what it actually allows — and what happens if you sit still.

August 31·4 min read
By Michael von Heesen — Licensed Insurance Agent, Brightwater Insurance Group

Every autumn, the volume of Medicare advertising goes up and the tone gets breathless. Underneath all of that noise is something genuinely simple: a yearly window, October 15 through December 7, when anyone with Medicare can make changes to how they receive their coverage. It's called the Annual Enrollment Period, and understanding what it actually allows — and what it doesn't require — takes most of the stress out of it.

What the window actually is

The Annual Enrollment Period is the same for everyone with Medicare, every year: October 15 through December 7. Any change you make during it generally takes effect on January 1 of the new plan year. That's the whole calendar. It isn't tied to your birthday, your enrollment anniversary, or anything personal — it's one shared window for reviewing and, if you choose, adjusting your coverage.

What you can do during it

The window allows three broad kinds of moves:

  • Switch from one Medicare Advantage plan to another. This is the most common change people make in the fall.
  • Move between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage — in either direction.
  • Join, drop, or switch a standalone Part D prescription drug plan.

One mechanic worth knowing: if you make more than one change during the window, the last valid choice you submit before December 7 is generally the one that takes effect. You're not locked in by your first move while the window is still open.

What happens if you do nothing

Nothing dramatic. Your current plan generally rolls into the new year automatically — there's no annual re-enrollment paperwork just to keep what you have. But there's a quiet catch: staying put isn't the same as staying the same. Plans adjust their provider networks, drug lists, and benefits from one year to the next, so the plan that continues in January may not behave exactly like the one you had in December. Doing nothing is a perfectly good decision when it's an informed one.

The one letter worth reading: your ANOC

That's why September matters more than October. Around now, plans mail every member an Annual Notice of Change (ANOC) — a plain summary of what will be different on January 1: costs, network, drug coverage, benefits. Ten unhurried minutes with that letter tells you whether this fall needs anything from you at all. If nothing meaningful is changing and your doctors and medications are still covered, your review may honestly be over before the window even opens.

A calm way to use September

Preparation isn't picking anything. It's three short lists: the doctors you want to keep, your medications with their dosages, and the pharmacy you prefer. When the ANOC arrives, you check it against those lists. That's the entire homework, and it's the same preparation whether you end up changing plans or happily keeping the one you have.

What the window is not

It isn't a test, and it isn't your only chance ever. If you enroll in a Medicare Advantage plan and have second thoughts, the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period — January 1 through March 31 — generally allows one further change. Special Enrollment Periods also exist year-round for qualifying life events, like moving out of your plan's service area. The fall window is simply the most convenient, lowest-friction time to make a considered change, because everyone's changes line up neatly with the new plan year.

The bottom line

October 15 to December 7 is an option, not an obligation. Read the letter your plan sends you, check it against your doctors and medications, and decide from facts. For many people the right answer will be "keep what I have" — and reaching that answer deliberately is exactly what the window is for.

Do I have to re-enroll in Medicare every fall?

No. If you do nothing during the Annual Enrollment Period, your current plan generally continues into the new year automatically. There is no annual paperwork required just to keep the coverage you already have.

When do changes made during the Annual Enrollment Period take effect?

Changes made during the October 15 to December 7 window generally take effect on January 1 of the new plan year.

What if I change my mind after submitting a change?

During the window itself, the last valid choice you submit before December 7 is generally the one that takes effect. And if you end up in a Medicare Advantage plan and have second thoughts, the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period from January 1 to March 31 generally allows one further change.

Is the Annual Enrollment Period the same as my Initial Enrollment Period?

No. Your Initial Enrollment Period is personal — a window built around the month you turn 65. The Annual Enrollment Period is the same calendar window for everyone who already has Medicare, running October 15 through December 7 every year.
