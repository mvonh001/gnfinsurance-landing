What Miami Seniors Should Know This Month (November 2026)

Four Medicare and Social Security stories from the past month worth a Miami senior's attention — each linked to the public source it came from, none of them a reason to panic.

November 2·5 min read
By Michael von Heesen — Licensed Insurance Agent, Brightwater Insurance Group

This is our monthly filter: a short, calm summary of what actually happened in Medicare over the past month, drawn from public sources you can check yourself, with a link to each one. October brought a Florida fraud conviction with a familiar pattern, a federal warning about the flood of insurance advertising this time of year, and Social Security's yearly benefit announcement. Nothing here is a reason to change your coverage; it's simply worth knowing.

A Florida jury convicts in an unwanted-brace scheme

On October 27, the Justice Department announced that a federal jury in the Middle District of Florida convicted a Gainesville medical-equipment company owner in a scheme that billed Medicare about 8.6 million dollars for orthotic braces. According to the release, marketing firms reached beneficiaries through high-pressure, manipulative telemarketing calls, then obtained prescriptions signed by practitioners who never examined the patients — or forged outright with stolen doctor credentials. The braces were shipped to people who neither wanted nor needed them, and a medical expert testified they could be dangerous for older patients. Sentencing is scheduled for January.

The pattern is worth recognizing because it keeps coming back: a phone call offering a "free" brace or supply, a few questions about your aches and pains, then a box at the door. Medical equipment should start with your own doctor, not a cold call. If a package arrives that you didn't order, or your Medicare Summary Notice lists equipment you never received, call 1-800-MEDICARE.

Two enrollment seasons, twice the advertising

Medicare's Annual Enrollment Period is already under way, and on November 1 the Health Insurance Marketplace opened its own enrollment window, which runs through January 15. On October 29, the Federal Trade Commission published a consumer alert about the ads, calls, and texts that come with it. The FTC notes that some pitches impersonate the government or well-known insurers, some "plans" turn out to be medical discount cards rather than insurance, and some offer far less coverage than advertised. One detail is especially useful: prerecorded marketing robocalls about health insurance are illegal, so a recorded voice pitching coverage is a strong sign you're dealing with a scam. Misleading ads and illegal robocalls can be reported at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. The Marketplace window matters mostly to family members under 65; if you're on Medicare, questions about your own coverage belong with Medicare, your plan, or a licensed agent you chose to contact.

Social Security announces its cost-of-living adjustment

On October 14, Social Security set its annual cost-of-living adjustment at 3.6 percent, based on September's inflation figures. The increase begins with benefits paid in January; people receiving Supplemental Security Income see it starting December 31. If your Medicare Part B premium is deducted from your Social Security payment, the change you actually notice will also reflect next year's Part B premium, which the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services announces separately. Social Security mails each beneficiary a notice showing the new amount, and you can also see it in your my Social Security account. One caution: scammers like to use COLA season as a hook, with calls or texts asking you to "confirm" your information to receive the increase. The raise is automatic — there is nothing to confirm.

November on the calendar

The Annual Enrollment Period continues through December 7, with any changes taking effect January 1. If you've already reviewed your Annual Notice of Change and you're comfortable with your coverage, there's nothing more you need to do. If you'd like unbiased help, Florida's free SHINE program offers Medicare counseling through the Department of Elder Affairs at 1-800-963-5337.

The bottom line

This month's stories share one lesson: legitimate help rarely arrives uninvited. Equipment comes through your own doctor, real enrollment help comes from sources you contact yourself, and benefit increases arrive without anyone asking for your information. Keep that in mind, and the busiest season of the Medicare year gets a lot quieter.
