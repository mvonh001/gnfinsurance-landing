Snowbirds and Medicare: What Splitting the Year Between States Means for Your Coverage

Medicare doesn't mind that you have two homes — but each part of it behaves differently when you're away. Here's how it actually works.

September 14·5 min read
By Michael von Heesen — Licensed Insurance Agent, Brightwater Insurance Group

Every fall, South Florida fills back up. People who spend summers up north return for the winter, and plenty of Miami residents make the reverse trip each spring. If you're one of them, you've probably wondered how Medicare feels about a life lived in two places. The short answer: Medicare itself is fine with it. The longer answer is that each piece of your coverage handles travel a little differently, and one address quietly decides most of it.

The address that runs the show

You have one Medicare record, and it's tied to one permanent address — the one on file with Social Security. That address decides which Medicare Advantage and Part D plans you can enroll in, because those plans are offered county by county. Your second home, however long you stay there, doesn't create a second set of options. So the first question to settle isn't "which plan?" It's "which home is my permanent residence?" Everything else follows from that answer.

Original Medicare: the same everywhere

Part A and Part B have no network. Any doctor or hospital in the United States that accepts Medicare will generally treat you the same way whether you're in Miami or Michigan. For people who split the year, this is the simplest part of the picture — it just works in both places.

Medigap: travels with Original Medicare

A Medigap policy sits on top of Original Medicare, so it generally follows the same rule: if a provider accepts Original Medicare, your standardized Medigap coverage generally applies there too. There's no separate network to check in your second state. The policy is priced based on where you permanently live, which brings us back to that one address again.

Medicare Advantage: the part that needs a conversation

Medicare Advantage plans are built around a service area and a provider network. That has three practical consequences for snowbirds:

  • Emergencies and urgently needed care are generally covered anywhere in the country, in either home.
  • Routine care away from home — a regular checkup, a follow-up visit — depends on the plan. Some plan types allow out-of-network care at a different cost share; others generally don't cover routine care outside the network at all.
  • Time away has limits. Plans generally have rules about how many consecutive months you can be outside the service area before they must disenroll you. Some plans offer a visitor or traveler program that extends that window and provides coverage in certain other areas. This is exactly the kind of detail to ask your plan about in writing before you leave.

None of this makes Medicare Advantage wrong for someone with two homes. It means the question "how does this plan handle the months I'm away?" belongs at the very top of your list, not the bottom.

Part D: same drug list, different pharmacies

Your prescription drug plan's formulary — the list of covered medications — doesn't change when you cross a state line. What changes is which pharmacies are in network. Many plans include national chains and a mail-order option, which is often the easiest route for a long seasonal stay. Before you travel, look up an in-network pharmacy near your second home and, if you use mail order, confirm the plan can ship to that address.

Seasonal stay or permanent move? Be honest with yourself

Spending five months in Florida is a seasonal stay. Selling the house up north and changing your address with Social Security is a move. The distinction matters because a genuine change of permanent residence generally opens a Special Enrollment Period — a window to pick a plan available in your new county. A seasonal stay generally doesn't. Pick the home that is truly your permanent one and build your coverage around it.

A short checklist before you pack

  • Confirm which address Social Security has as your permanent residence.
  • If you have Medicare Advantage, ask your plan how it covers routine care away from home and what its limit is on time outside the service area.
  • Find an in-network pharmacy near your second home, or set up mail order.
  • Carry your plan cards and the plan's member phone number — the one on the back of the card.
  • Keep your medication list current, with dosages, so any new provider can pick up where the last one left off.

The bottom line

Living in two states doesn't break Medicare. Original Medicare and Medigap generally go wherever you go; Medicare Advantage and Part D go with you too, but with rules worth knowing in advance. Settle the question of your permanent address, ask your plan the travel questions before the trip, and the rest of the winter is yours.

Can I have Medicare coverage in two states at once?

You have one Medicare record tied to one permanent address — the one on file with Social Security. Original Medicare itself works in every state. Plan options such as Medicare Advantage and Part D are based on the county of that permanent address, so you generally can't hold one plan in each state.

Does a Medicare Advantage plan cover me while I'm in my other state?

Emergency and urgently needed care are generally covered anywhere in the United States. Routine care away from home depends on the plan's network and rules, and some plans offer a visitor or traveler program for extended stays. Plans also generally have limits on how long you can be out of the service area, so it's worth asking your plan directly before you leave.

Does spending the winter in Florida count as moving?

Generally not, as long as your permanent address stays the same. A seasonal stay is different from a permanent move. A genuine change of permanent residence generally opens a Special Enrollment Period, because your plan options are tied to where you live.

Will my Part D drug plan work at a pharmacy in my second state?

Your drug list stays the same wherever you are. What changes is which pharmacies are in your plan's network. Many plans include national chains and mail-order options, so checking for an in-network pharmacy near your second home before you travel is the practical step.
