What's Changing for Medicare Plans in 2027

Plans are allowed to change every year — premium, drug coverage, and network included. Here's what that generally looks like.

September 1·4 min read
By Michael von Heesen — Licensed Insurance Agent, Brightwater Insurance Group

Every year, Medicare plans are allowed to change. Premiums, drug coverage, provider networks — a plan can look different on January 1 than it did the year before, even if you never asked for anything to change. That pattern holds again heading into 2027.

Why plans change every year

Insurance companies that offer Medicare Advantage and Part D drug plans refile their plan details with Medicare each year. As part of that annual process, they're permitted to adjust pricing, coverage, and the doctors and pharmacies included in the plan.

That's true whether a plan is popular or not, and whether you've been on it one year or ten. There's no version of Medicare Advantage or Part D that's frozen in place indefinitely.

This isn't unique to any single insurance company. It's simply how the annual filing process works across the industry, which is why it's worth building the habit of checking rather than watching for one plan in particular.

What can actually change

The list of what's allowed to move is longer than most people expect:

  • The monthly premium — up, down, or to $0.
  • Deductibles and copays for hospital stays, specialist visits, and prescriptions.
  • Which drugs are covered, and which pricing tier they fall into.
  • Which doctors, specialists, and pharmacies are in the plan's network.
  • Extra benefits, like dental, vision, hearing, or an over-the-counter allowance.

What this means for 2027 specifically

For 2027, the same annual process applies. Any changes a plan makes take effect on January 1, 2027, and they're specific to that plan — not a general, across-the-board shift that applies to everyone equally.

That's exactly why there's no way to know what applies to you without checking your own plan's paperwork this fall. A neighbor's plan changing, or staying the same, doesn't tell you anything about yours.

What generally doesn't change

Not everything is up for revision every year. Your right to Medicare itself, the broad structure of Parts A, B, C, and D, and your protections during Open Enrollment all stay in place from one year to the next.

What moves is specific to each plan: its price, its drug list, and its network. That's the part worth checking — not the system underneath it.

The letter that will tell you

Every plan mails an Annual Notice of Change letter, generally by September 30, laying out what's different for the coming year. It typically arrives with the rest of the mail and is easy to set aside without opening.

We'll walk through exactly which lines in that letter matter most, and which you can skim past, in next week's post.

Why this is worth ten minutes now, not in December

Open Enrollment — October 15 through December 7 — is the window when you can actually act on anything that changed. It closes on the same date every year, with no extensions.

Reading the changes early just means you're not doing it for the first time during the busiest, most compressed weeks of the year.

The bottom line

Nothing about a plan changing is unusual or alarming — it's simply how the annual process works. The only question worth asking is whether this year's changes still fit your doctors, your medications, and your budget.

Will my plan definitely change for 2027?

Not necessarily. Some plans change very little from one year to the next. But Medicare allows plans to change their premium, drug coverage, and network each year, so it's worth checking rather than assuming nothing moved.

When do any 2027 changes actually start?

January 1, 2027, for any Medicare Advantage or Part D changes that take effect. You'll find out what's changing on your specific plan before Open Enrollment (October 15 to December 7, 2026) begins.

How will I know what's changing on my plan?

Your plan mails an Annual Notice of Change letter, generally by September 30 each year, summarizing what's different for the coming year.

Do I have to switch plans if something changed?

No. You can stay on your current plan if it still works for you. Reviewing what changed just means the decision to stay is one you made on purpose, not by default.
